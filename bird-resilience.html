Climate resilience of flagship bird communities

A comparative study of bird community resilience and future climate refugia across China and the United States.

Research question

How does climate change influence the spatial resilience of flagship bird communities, and where may future climate refugia occur in China and the United States?

Why this question?

Climate change is reshaping species distributions and ecological communities. However, species richness alone does not fully describe whether a community can remain resilient as environmental conditions change. This project therefore evaluates bird community resilience by integrating species distributions, functional redundancy, and response diversity. Future climate projections are then compared with current resilience patterns to identify potential climate refugia.

Methodology

Species distribution models were developed to estimate the environmental suitability of each flagship bird species under current and future climate conditions. These predictions were then combined to quantify bird community resilience using functional redundancy and response diversity. An XGBoost–SHAP framework was subsequently used to investigate the environmental drivers of community resilience.

Future climate refugia

The next stage of the analysis compares current community resilience with projected future resilience under climate change. Areas were identified as potential climate refugia based on how their resilience changes between current conditions and future climate scenarios.

From resilience to refugia

Rather than treating future climate refugia as a separate analysis, this step builds directly on the current and future ACR maps. The change in community resilience provides a basis for identifying areas that may retain or gain resilience under future climate conditions.

Stable refugia

Areas with high current resilience that remain highly resilient under future climate conditions.

Transitional refugia

Areas that have lower current resilience but may develop higher resilience under future climate conditions.

Potential refugia

Areas with high current resilience that may experience some decline while still remaining relevant for future conservation.

The resulting map highlights potential areas for biodiversity persistence under projected climate conditions across China and the United States.

Conservation implications

Combining community resilience with future climate refugia identification provides a spatial framework for understanding where bird communities may be better able to persist under climate change.

From resilience to conservation

The results suggest that conservation planning should not focus only on current species richness. Areas with high current resilience, areas likely to maintain resilience in the future, and areas that may gain resilience under changing climatic conditions can represent different conservation priorities. Understanding these spatial differences may help inform climate-adaptive biodiversity conservation in both China and the United States.

This project changed the way I think about biodiversity. A map of species richness can tell us where species are, but resilience asks a different question:

What happens when the environment changes — and where might biodiversity persist?
