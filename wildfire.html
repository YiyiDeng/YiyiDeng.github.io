RESEARCH · 02 · WILDFIRE & BIODIVERSITY

Wildfire impacts on biodiversity in California

Using high-resolution satellite imagery and spatial analysis to investigate how wildfire severity relates to biodiversity change.

RESEARCH PRESENTED AT AOGS

The question

Wildfires can rapidly transform ecosystems, but their ecological effects are not spatially uniform. This project investigated how differences in burn severity were associated with changes in biodiversity across three California wildfire events.

Study area

The study focused on three major wildfire events in California:

Palisades California wildfire
Eaton California wildfire
Hughes California wildfire
Study area showing the three California wildfire events.

From fire to biodiversity

Rather than treating the analysis as a single technical workflow, I approached the research as four connected questions: Where did the fire occur? How severe was it? How did biodiversity respond? And why did those responses vary across space?

01 · MAP THE FIRE

Where did the fire occur?

I used Sentinel-2 satellite imagery and a U-Net model to delineate the burned areas of the three wildfire events at 10-meter spatial resolution.

Sentinel-2 imagery → U-Net burn-area delineation
02 · MEASURE SEVERITY

How severe was the disturbance?

Differenced Normalized Burn Ratio (dNBR) was used to quantify the intensity of ecological disturbance across the burned landscape.

Burned area → dNBR burn severity
03 · TRACK BIODIVERSITY

How did species respond?

I examined changes in species richness across amphibians, reptiles, and mammals to understand how different groups responded to wildfire severity.

Burn severity → Species richness change
04 · EXPLAIN SPATIAL PATTERNS

Why did responses vary?

Multiscale Geographically Weighted Regression (MGWR) was used to investigate how the relationship between wildfire severity and biodiversity varied across space.

Local ecological response → Spatially varying relationships
