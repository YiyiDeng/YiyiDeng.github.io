EcoGuard

Turning environmental awareness into practical change within my school community.

From awareness
to action

Through EcoGuard, I worked with my school community to promote more sustainable everyday practices. One project focused on reducing single-use plastic packaging in the school cafeteria and encouraging a transition toward more sustainable alternatives.

Small changes can become visible changes.

Rather than thinking about sustainability only as a large-scale environmental problem, I wanted to explore how everyday decisions within a community could also contribute to a more sustainable campus.

Why packaging?

In my proposal, I estimated that the cafeteria used approximately 66 takeaway containers each day, or about 10,560 over a school year. These figures were conservative estimates based on the staff takeaway patterns described in the proposal. The project explored ways to reduce reliance on disposable plastic packaging and introduce alternative containers.

Packaging in practice

These photos document sample cafeteria packaging formats considered as part of the initiative. The proposal discussed alternatives including corn-starch-based and natural sugarcane-fiber containers.

The proposal

The proposal outlined a phased approach: an initial school purchase of alternative containers, an optional staff contribution toward the upgrade cost, and cafeteria-side posters explaining the benefits of reducing disposable packaging.

What I learned

Environmental change does not always begin with large scientific projects. Sometimes it begins with a conversation, a different choice, or a small change in the environment immediately around us. EcoGuard gave me an opportunity to consider environmental action from a community perspective — and to think about how research and awareness can be translated into practical proposals.
